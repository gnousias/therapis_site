Do you know what is pelvic floor?

← All articles

15 October 2026

While we often focus on our musculoskeletal health, one critical system of our body remains on the sidelines of public discussion: the pelvic floor. It is a complex of muscles and ligaments at the base of the pelvis that acts like a “hammock,” supporting vital organs (bladder, bowel, uterus) and ensuring control of urination and sexual function. Despite the silence surrounding it, the problems related to it are extremely widespread.

Physiotherapy as a "Gold Standard"

Many patients are mistakenly led to believe that the only solution is surgery or medication, while others believe that there is no solution and they must learn to live with it. However, the scientific community now has a different approach. In particular, the International Continence Society (ICS), which is the leading global scientific organization involved in the study, diagnosis and treatment of lower urinary tract dysfunctions, including incontinence, confirms through extensive studies that Pelvic Floor Physiotherapy should be the first-line treatment for stress incontinence and mild prolapse. Research shows that specialized muscle retraining through physical therapy has success rates approaching 80%, reducing the need for invasive methods. Specialized pelvic floor physiotherapy uses tools such as biofeedback and electrical stimulation, combined with a personalized exercise program, to restore function to the area.

The most common symptoms of "stress incontinence"...

If you notice urine leaking when you cough, laugh, exercise, or lift weights, after pregnancy and childbirth, or if the hormonal changes of menopause have brought new challenges to your daily life, remember: you are not alone. These symptoms are extremely common, but they are not “normal” or something you should just accept. Get an expert’s advice. Don’t hesitate to break the silence and see a pelvic floor physiotherapist or your doctor for a personalized assessment. The message is clear: Incontinence and pain are not a “normal consequence” of aging or childbirth. They are treatable conditions. Early detection is the key to regaining control and confidence and the first step to a life of freedom and movement.
